## The two surfaces

- **Web/Google:** name + domain + title tag carry keywords. An exact-match domain (learnchineseforkids.com) still helps for the exact query but the SERP is listicle-dominated; realistically you rank via content pages, and the name's job is to be self-explanatory in a title tag.
- **App Store:** the 30-char name and 30-char subtitle are the two strongest keyword fields (words are indexed individually; no need to repeat a word across both). Pattern winners use "Brand: descriptor" (Lingokids: Games for Toddlers).
- **Honest evaluation of pure-descriptive:** a name that IS the generic query ("Chinese for Kids") can never be trademarked, can't build navigational/brand search (Google treats it as generic intent, so you compete with every listicle forever for your own name), and Apple has rejected keyword-string names in the Kids Category. The best answer to the clarity directive is a **fully descriptive but distinctive word pairing** — 100% plain-language, zero decoding required, yet ownable as a mark. That is what candidate 1 is.

## Recommendation

**Mandarin & Math for Kids** (short form "Mandarin & Math"). It is the only candidate that satisfies the clarity directive on its face — a parent reading the name knows exactly what the app teaches and who it's for — while still being ownable: the alliterative pairing is distinctive, both domains are unregistered today, and no App Store or brand collision exists. It carries "mandarin," "math," and "kids" in the name and lets the subtitle add "learn chinese words numbers," covering both the "chinese" and "mandarin" query phrasings.

Runner-up if maximum Google exact-match matters more than ownability: **Learn Chinese for Kids** (both domains free), accepting no trademark, permanent SERP competition with listicles for its own name, and Kids Category name-review risk.
